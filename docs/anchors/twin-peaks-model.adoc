Full Name:: Twin Peaks model, from Bashar Nuseibeh, "Weaving Together Requirements and Architectures" (_IEEE Computer_ 34(3), March 2001, pp. 115--119, https://doi.org/10.1109/2.910904[doi:10.1109/2.910904]).

Also known as:: Twin Peaks, the Twin Peaks of requirements and architecture

[discrete]
== *Core Concepts*:

Two Peaks, Developed Together:: Requirements engineering and architectural design are separate activities, but they run concurrently and feed each other. Neither is finished before the other starts.

Equal Status:: The name reflects that both peaks are equally high: the requirements specification and the architecture carry the same weight. Requirements do not simply "flow down" into an architecture.

Against Frozen Requirements:: Nuseibeh's starting point is the waterfall habit of picking one side as the starting point, which produces artificially frozen requirements documents for the next phase, and architectures that resist the requirement changes they meet later.

A Partial Spiral:: The model is a simplified, finer-grained form of the spiral life cycle. Each turn refines the requirements and the architecture a little further, in alternation.

Architecture Feeds Back Into Requirements:: Architectural decisions make requirements more concrete or force their re-evaluation. An option can make a requirement cheap, expensive or impossible, so the feedback runs in both directions.

Stable Yet Adaptable:: The aim is an architecture that stays stable while the requirements around it keep changing, because the decisions that matter were made with the requirements in view.

[discrete]
== *When to Use*:

* Projects where requirements are still moving while the architecture has to start
* Pushing back on "finish the spec first, then design" in a plan or in an AI agent's workflow
* Linking quality requirements and architectural decisions, e.g. a `req42` goal or quality scenario to an `arc42` decision
* Driving a model: "work in Twin Peaks style -- after each requirement, name the architectural consequence, and after each architectural option, name the requirement it puts at risk" asks for the alternation, not a two-phase document

[discrete]
== *Common Misunderstandings*:

* ❌ "Twin Peaks" alone -- without "model" or "Nuseibeh", the term is ambiguous with the TV series; use the qualified form
* ❌ "Requirements first, then architecture, but iterate" -- the model asks for concurrent work, not alternating phases with hand-overs
* ❌ "Architecture follows from the requirements" -- in Twin Peaks the architecture also changes the requirements
* ✓ "Refine requirements and architecture together, and let each constrain the other"

[discrete]
