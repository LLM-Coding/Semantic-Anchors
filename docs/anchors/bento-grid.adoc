Full Name:: Bento Grid -- a layout of rounded tiles in different sizes on one grid, each tile holding one piece of content

Also known as:: Bento box layout, bento UI

[discrete]
== *Core Concepts*:

* *One grid, tiles of different sizes.* Tiles span one or more columns and rows -- 2 × 2, 2 × 1, 1 × 1 -- so the layout is uneven but still aligned.
* *Size carries the hierarchy.* The most important item gets the largest tile; secondary items fill smaller ones around it.
* *One idea per tile.* Each compartment holds one feature, number, image or short statement, the way a bento box holds one dish per compartment.
* *Rounded cards with even gaps.* Tiles have generous corner radii and the same gap on every side, which makes the uneven sizes read as one system.
* *Rich tile content.* Tiles often show a product screenshot, a large figure or an icon rather than only text.
* *Built on CSS Grid.* Spanning columns and rows is what CSS Grid does natively; on narrow screens the tiles collapse into one or two columns.

Key Proponents:: No single originator. Many attribute it to Apple's promotional videos and keynote summary slides; some trace it further back to the tile layout of Microsoft's Metro design in Windows Phone 7 (https://bootcamp.uxdesign.cc/web-design-trend-bento-box-95814d99ac62["Web design trend: bento box"], UX Collective Bootcamp). The name comes from the Japanese bento lunch box with its divided compartments.

[discrete]
== *When to Use*:

* Feature sections on product landing pages, where a handful of features of unequal weight must fit one screen
* Portfolios, dashboards and "at a glance" summaries
* As a counter-instruction when generated layouts fall back on a uniform grid of identical cards

[discrete]
== *Common Misunderstandings*:

* *Treating any card grid as a bento grid.* A grid of identical cards is just a card grid. The varied tile sizes are what make it bento.
* *Using the bare "bento".* In our prior test on 2026-10-29, "What is bento?" resolved to Japanese food on all three tiers. Say "bento grid".
* *Expecting it by default.* In the same test, five of six baselines laid six features out as a uniform three-by-two grid. With "Use a bento grid." six of six runs mixed tile sizes, typically a 2 × 2 hero tile with smaller tiles around it.
* *Ignoring the narrow screen.* The composition depends on width; on a phone it becomes a stack, and the hierarchy has to survive that.

[discrete]
== *Criticism*:

* *Too much in one screen.* David Jaja warns in https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/bento-grids-in-web-design/["Bento Grids in Web Design"] (freeCodeCamp, 2024) that "there's a risk of cramming too much information into a single screen". He also names weak hierarchy when tiles look alike, choice overload and monotony when the grid is overused.
