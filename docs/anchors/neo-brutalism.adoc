Full Name:: Neo-Brutalism -- a visual style for web and UI design with thick black borders, hard offset shadows, flat saturated colours and blocky layouts

Also known as:: Neobrutalism, neubrutalism

[discrete]
== *Core Concepts*:

* *Thick black outlines.* Cards, buttons and images carry a solid black border of two to four pixels. The outline, not a soft shadow, separates an element from its background.
* *Hard offset shadows.* Shadows are solid black or dark blocks shifted a few pixels down and to the right, with no blur. On hover, an element often moves into its shadow to look pressed.
* *Flat, saturated colour.* Large areas of strong colour -- yellow, pink, mint, blue -- with no gradients. Black text sits on top.
* *Blocky, visible structure.* Layouts are built from rectangles with sharp or slightly rounded corners. The grid shows; nothing hides that the page is made of boxes.
* *Bold, plain type.* Heavy grotesque or monospace fonts, large headings, often in capitals.
* *Order under the noise.* Unlike raw brutalist web design, the style keeps a grid, a clear hierarchy and conventional navigation. The Nielsen Norman Group describes it as a more colourful and orderly descendant of brutalism and a reaction against sleek minimalism.

Key Proponents:: No single originator. The style spread as a community trend in product and web design; the Nielsen Norman Group defines it in https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/["Neobrutalism: Definition and Best Practices"] (Hayat Sheikh, 2025) and names Gumroad and Figma's brand refresh as examples

[discrete]
== *When to Use*:

* Landing pages, portfolios and marketing sites that should look loud, playful or deliberately unpolished
* Products for designers, developers and creative audiences, where an informal tone fits
* As a counter-instruction when generated designs drift towards soft gradients, rounded cards and blurred shadows

[discrete]
== *Common Misunderstandings*:

* *Confusing it with brutalist web design.* Raw brutalism ignores conventions on purpose. Neo-Brutalism keeps order, a grid and a clear hierarchy.
* *Reading high contrast as accessible.* Black outlines are high contrast, but saturated colour pairs can still fail text contrast checks.
* *Using it everywhere.* The playful look clashes with contexts that depend on trust, such as banking or health.
* *Using the bare "brutalism".* In our prior test on 2026-10-28, "What is brutalism?" resolved to architecture on all tiers. With "Use neo-brutalism." a bakery landing page got black borders and hard offset shadows in six of six runs; none of six baselines used either. The prefix is what reaches the web style.

[discrete]
== *Criticism*:

* *Loud styling can cost usability.* Hayat Sheikh warns in https://www.nngroup.com/articles/neobrutalism/["Neobrutalism: Definition and Best Practices"] (Nielsen Norman Group, 2025) that "without balance, it can overwhelm users and hinder accessibility". She advises two or three colours and neutral fonts for body text.
* *The parent style trades usability for attitude.* Kate Moran argues in https://www.nngroup.com/articles/brutalism-antidesign/["Brutalism and Antidesign"] (Nielsen Norman Group, 2017) that deliberately hard-to-read layouts conflict with getting tasks done: "nobody ever complains that a site is too easy to understand."
