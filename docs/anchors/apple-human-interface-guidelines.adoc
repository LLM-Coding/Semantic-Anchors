Full Name:: Apple Human Interface Guidelines -- Apple's design guidance for apps on its platforms, one document for all of them since 2022

Also known as:: HIG, Apple HIG

[discrete]
== *Core Concepts*:

* *Guiding themes.* Classic iOS guidance named clarity, deference and depth. The 2025 framing speaks of hierarchy, harmony and consistency.
* *Targets a finger can hit.* Controls need a minimum hit target of 44 × 44 points.
* *System type and symbols.* SF Pro and SF Symbols; Dynamic Type lets text follow the user's chosen size.
* *Familiar navigation.* Large titles, navigation bars, tab bars and inset grouped lists give apps a shared structure.
* *Respect the device.* Layouts stay inside safe areas; system colours adapt to dark mode.
* *Accessibility built in.* VoiceOver, Reduce Motion and Dynamic Type are part of the design, not an add-on.

Key Proponents:: Apple. The guidance goes back to _Human Interface Guidelines: The Apple Desktop Interface_ (Apple Computer / Addison-Wesley, 1987; https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102679991[Computer History Museum]). On 7 June 2022 Apple merged the per-platform guides into one HIG with a change log (https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=v8a3aetj[Apple Developer News])

[discrete]
== *When to Use*:

* Designing iPhone, iPad or Mac apps that should feel native. In our prior test the anchor made every run set 44-point targets for a settings screen, and most added a large title, Dynamic Type and inset grouped lists.
* Reviewing an app's controls for hit size, type scaling and dark mode
* Porting an Android app to Apple platforms, as a contrast to <<material-design-3,Material Design 3>>
* Together with <<wcag-22-aa,WCAG 2.2 AA>> when accessibility must also be checked against a standard

[discrete]
== *Common Misunderstandings*:

* *Mixing it up with the App Store rules.* The App Review Guidelines are a separate document. The HIG guides design; it does not decide approval.
* *Reading Liquid Glass as translucent content.* The material is meant for the layer of controls and navigation, not for the content itself.
* *Treating it as iOS-only.* Since 2022 it is one document for all of Apple's platforms.
* *Expecting the details from the baseline.* In our prior test on 2026-10-28, four of six baselines already chose SF Pro, but none set a 44-point target. With the anchor, six of six set 44-point targets, and five of six used a large title, Dynamic Type and inset grouped lists.

[discrete]
== *Criticism*:

* *Spectacle over usability.* Raluca Budiu, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/["Liquid Glass Is Cracked, and Usability Suffers in iOS 26"] (Nielsen Norman Group, 2025), finds that "Apple is prioritizing spectacle over usability". She reports that translucent controls hurt legibility, and points to distracting motion, cramped targets and hidden features.
* *Breaking its own rules.* Michal Langmajer asks in https://uxdesign.cc/did-apple-abandoned-its-own-design-heuristics-accessibility-principles-2d616ed7ace5["Did Apple abandon its own design heuristics & accessibility principles?"] (UX Collective) whether Apple has abandoned its own design heuristics; he argues that Liquid Glass contradicts the clarity-first principles of the HIG and WCAG contrast guidance.

[discrete]
== *Current Status*:

* *Liquid Glass is the current design language.* Apple announced it on 9 June 2025 for iOS 26, iPadOS 26, macOS Tahoe 26, watchOS 26 and tvOS 26: a translucent material that "reflects and refracts its surroundings" (https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/[Apple Newsroom]). Apple's https://developer.apple.com/design/whats-new/[change log] shows refinements in December 2025 and June 2026. At WWDC26 on 8 June 2026 Apple brought back a "Design principles" page and published kits for iOS, iPadOS and macOS 27; the latest entry is dated 13 October 2026. The training-data prior most likely serves the flat iOS look from before 2025. Ask for Liquid Glass by name if you want it.
